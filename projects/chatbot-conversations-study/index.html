What we're studying

A lot of people now turn to AI chatbots when something goes wrong between them and someone else. Chatbots are always available, never judge, and usually take your side.

That last part is what we want to understand. Research shows that AI chatbots tend to agree with and validate the person they're talking to, sometimes even when that person's account is one-sided. Researchers call this sycophancy. In a conflict, hearing only your side reflected back can feel good in the moment. We don't yet know how it relates to how people feel over time, how they see the other person, or where else they turn for support.

This study looks at real conversations people have already had with chatbots about interpersonal situations, alongside survey questions about wellbeing, relationships, and help-seeking. We're not testing you or judging how you use AI. There are no right answers.

Why real conversations? Most research on chatbot sycophancy uses made-up prompts in a lab. How people actually talk to chatbots about their own lives is messier and more personal, and that's exactly why it matters to study it carefully, and why we built tools to keep you in control of what you share.

About the Chrome extension

Chatbot sites don't make it easy to save a full conversation in a clean, readable form. The LLM Chat Exporter is a formatting tool that runs only on your computer. It turns a conversation on your screen into a file you can review, redact, and then choose to upload.

It works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini in the Chrome browser on a computer.

What it does

  • Scrolls through the conversation you choose, so the whole chat is captured, not just what fits on screen
  • Lets you redact any message or part of a message before saving
  • Adds your participant ID so we can match the file to your survey without your name
  • Saves a file to your own computer

What it doesn't do

  • Send anything to us, the chatbot company, or anyone else
  • Read conversations you don't open and export yourself
  • Run on other websites or track your browsing
  • Ask for your chatbot password or account login

What happens to your data

Your conversation only leaves your computer when you upload it, and only after you've had the chance to review and redact it.

What we collect
Your survey answers and the conversation file(s) you upload, after your redactions, linked by a participant ID.
Your identity
Your name isn't attached to your responses. We only ask for your email to send you the amazon giftcard and if you opt in for our optional interview.
Where it's stored
All your information is stored in Qualtrics, a Penn State-approved secure storage system.
Who can see it
Only the study's research team. In papers and talks, we report patterns across many people. State whether short de-identified excerpts may be quoted.
Changing your mind
Taking part is completely voluntary. You can stop at any time.

Questions people ask

Do I have to be a Penn State student?

No, anyone in the United States can participate in this study.

I mostly use the chatbot app on my phone. Can I still take part?

Yes, as long as you can sign in to the same chatbot account in Chrome on a computer. Most chatbots sync your past conversations to their website, so you can export them from there.

Will the chatbot company know I'm in this study?

No. This study is run independently by researchers at Penn State, and the extension doesn't share anything with the chatbot company.

What if my conversation has really personal details in it?

That's expected, and it's why the extension lets you redact anything before saving. You choose what's in the file, and you can review it before uploading.

Do I need to keep the extension installed?

No. Once you've uploaded your file, you can remove it from Chrome like any other extension.

Who reviewed this study?

This study was reviewed by the Penn State Institutional Review Board (IRB), protocol STUDY00029720. For questions about your rights as a participant, you can contact Penn State's Human Research Protection Program.
