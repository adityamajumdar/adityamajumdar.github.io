My research examines how AI systems affect individuals, organizations, and communities, with a particular focus on understanding what makes these systems effective and responsible in practice. I study both how people interact with AI and how organizations navigate the challenges of developing, adopting, and deploying AI technologies. To investigate these questions, I combine qualitative research methods with computational approaches, including large language model evaluation, statistical modeling, and optimization. My goal is to translate these insights into practical evaluation methods, frameworks, and tools that help align AI systems with human needs and societal priorities.

<h3>Bio</h3>

Aditya Majumdar is a PhD student in Informatics at Penn State, advised by <a href="http://amulyayadav.com/">Amulya Yadav</a> in the AI for Social Impact lab. Aditya's research sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction, responsible AI, and AI for social impact. It examines why AI built for nonprofits and public agencies so often fails to reach the people it was meant for, how sycophantic chatbots affect users' wellbeing and moral judgment, and how reliably large language models handle high-stakes questions such as health advice. This work has appeared at FAccT 2026, including a first-author study of the <a href="/projects/facct2026-hardness/">barriers to deploying AI for social impact</a> and a paper <a href="/projects/llm-health-advice/">evaluating LLM health advice</a>, and has been covered by MIT Technology Review. Partners have included the Allegheny County Department of Human Services, the Naandi Foundation in India, and World Hope International in the Philippines. Aditya is supported by an NSF LinDiv Fellowship, was previously a DAAD RISE scholar at the University of Konstanz, and holds a B.S. in Computer Science from Penn State, cum laude. Outside research, Aditya served as Vice President of <a href="https://www.tedxpsu.com/">TEDxPSU</a> and as a camp counselor for <a href="https://www.kesem.org/">Kesem Central PA</a>.
